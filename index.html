Where this comes from

OpenShift Games is a derivative work of K8s Games, the game that put a Kubernetes cluster in 3D on the web.

K8s Games by Rohit did something the documentation never does: it made a cluster a place. You could build a Deployment, watch it schedule, kill a pod and see the controller put it back — in a browser, with no install. It is Apache-2.0 licensed, and its source, spirit and interface are the foundation this project grew from.

OpenShift Games keeps that idea and points it at the exam: a full OpenShift 4.22 platform model with Projects, Routes, S2I builds, ImageStreams, SCCs, RBAC evaluation, quotas, LimitRanges and OLM operators, and every campaign level mapped to the official EX280V422 objective groups. The homage is intentional and permanent.

  • OpenShift 4.22 semanticsProjects instead of namespaces, Routes instead of Ingress, BuildConfigs and ImageStreams for source builds.
  • EX280 objective mappingNine groups and 55 bullets tracked across levels, timed labs and quick labs.
  • A real oc CLInew-project, new-app, expose, set, rollout, adm policy and auth can-i, with honest failures.
  • Platform operationsQuotas, LimitRanges, ClusterResourceQuota, Operators, SCCs and LoadBalancers that behave like the platform.
  • Twenty levels, five chaptersFrom your first Project to a full production platform build.
  • Twelve timed labsSpeed drills from CrashLoopBackOff to edge TLS Routes, scored against the clock.

Built for a desktop

The 3D cluster, the oc terminal and the console panels need a real keyboard and a wide screen to teach the way they should. At this size the simulation is hard to use.

Open OpenShift Games on a laptop or desktop, or continue on this device at your own pace.
